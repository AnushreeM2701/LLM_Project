% ---------- Abstract (≤200 words per the MSc outline) ----------
\fancyhead[L]{\bfseries Abstract}
\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}

Large Language Models (LLMs) are frequently evaluated on mathematical
reasoning benchmarks using only final-answer accuracy, which provides
limited insight into their reasoning processes. This dissertation
assesses three LLMs -- Gemini Flash Lite (\texttt{gemini-flash-lite-latest},
resolving to \texttt{gemini-3.5-flash-lite}), GPT-OSS-120B
(\texttt{openai/gpt-oss-120b}, accessed via Groq), and Mistral Large
(\texttt{mistral-large-latest}, resolving to Mistral Large 3,
\texttt{mistral-large-2512}) -- by applying both Chain-of-Thought (CoT)
and Tree-of-Thought (ToT) prompting to 131 mathematics problems from
Hendrycks MATH and the American Invitational Mathematics Examination
(AIME), yielding 786 responses. The study addresses three primary
questions. First, it analyses the types of reasoning errors produced by
each model, finding that Combinatorial Counting Error is the most
prevalent across all three, with a Chi-square test indicating no
significant difference in error distributions. Second, it investigates
the relationship between reasoning length and correctness, revealing
that for Mistral Large, incorrect Hard-tier responses involve more
steps than correct ones, a pattern not observed in Gemini or
GPT-OSS-120B. Third, it evaluates whether ToT prompting enhances
correctness relative to CoT. McNemar's Exact Test demonstrates that ToT
significantly increases accuracy for GPT-OSS-120B and Mistral Large,
but not for Gemini, despite ToT incurring approximately four times the
computational cost. These findings indicate that LLMs vary in their
reasoning errors and in the extent to which they benefit from different
prompting strategies, and that relying solely on accuracy would obscure
these distinctions.

\vspace{30pt}
\emph{\textbf{Keywords:} Large Language Models, Mathematical Reasoning, Chain-of-Thought, Tree-of-Thought, Error Analysis}
